Path of the Glacier Trail: A Short Walk to Mount Edith Cavell

A short and popular walk near Jasper delivers an enormous view: Mount Edith Cavell, Angel Glacier, Cavell Glacier and a cold meltwater lake scattered with pieces of ice.

By the Cool TravelPal TeamPublished July 25, 20265 min read
Route taken

A short walk with a huge payoff

Some trails ask you to work for the view. This one lets you see the reason for the drive almost immediately. Path of the Glacier is in the Mount Edith Cavell area of Jasper National Park, and it is one of those popular stops where the walk itself is simple enough for many visitors.

On my visit, the trail felt short and straightforward. There is a little climb near the beginning, then the landscape opens and the mountain starts to dominate the frame. I kept thinking that the trail had an unusually generous ratio of effort to scenery. You do not need a full day of hiking to stand in front of a real glacier.

The glacier and the floating ice

The lake below the glacier is what made this stop stay in my memory. The water has a cold green-blue color, and small chunks of ice drift near the rocky shore. They look delicate from a distance; the surrounding cliffs make it clear how powerful the environment really is.

My favorite moment was simply standing there and watching the ice move. The scene changes slowly, almost imperceptibly, while the mountain remains completely still. That contrast gave the place a calm feeling, even though Parks Canada is right to warn visitors about falling rock and ice in the area.

The small details in the landscape

The trail crosses a rocky landscape that was recently covered by glacial ice. The bare stones and young vegetation make the retreat of the glacier visible in a way a sign cannot quite explain. You can see the mountain’s story in layers: old rock, fresh ice, meltwater, and the first plants returning to the ground.

That is why I would recommend this trail even to visitors who do not think of themselves as hikers. The walk is popular, the distance is modest, and the reward is a close view of a glacier rather than a distant white patch on a skyline. Bring time to stop at the viewpoint. The view deserves more than a quick photograph before heading back to the car.

Current planning notes

Important current status: Parks Canada’s Mount Edith Cavell page says Cavell Road and the Mount Edith Cavell area remain closed for the 2026 season while the park assesses conditions and plans rehabilitation work. My photographs are from an earlier visit; do not use this story as proof that the trail is open now.

  • Trail facts: Parks Canada lists Trail 41 Path of the Glacier as moderate, 1.6 km return, 70 m elevation gain/loss, and about one hour. The trail begins at the end of Cavell Road, starts with stairs and a paved section, and continues to a viewpoint over Cavell Pond.
  • Access when it reopens: the approach uses a narrow, winding 14 km Cavell Road from Highway 93A. The current page lists a maximum vehicle length of 7.5 m (25 feet); trailers and large motorhomes are not allowed.
  • Season: Parks Canada says access is prohibited from November 1 through May 15 for Tonquin caribou conservation. Always check for additional closures, wildfire recovery work and road updates.
  • Safety: stay on the designated trail and viewpoints. Falling rock and ice remain hazards, and approaching the pond edge beneath Angel Glacier is not advised. House-size blocks of ice can fall without warning.
  • Conditions: check the Jasper trail-conditions page, road updates, weather and wildfire notices before setting out.
  • Dogs and fragile ground: follow current dog restrictions, keep to the trail, and do not pick plants or feed wildlife. Vegetation grows slowly at this elevation.

For the official details, read Parks Canada’s Mount Edith Cavell area page and its current trail conditions. When the road reopens, those pages should be your source for access, conditions, restrictions and the official Edith Cavell map.

My final verdict is simple: Path of the Glacier is a beautiful, efficient Jasper stop. The walk is short, the setting is dramatic, and the ice in the lake makes the glacier feel alive. I would happily return when the road is open again.
